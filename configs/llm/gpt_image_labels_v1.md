You are an experienced musculoskeletal radiologist. You will see every image of one knee MRI study, grouped by series. There is no clinical history and no report.

For each of the twelve labels below, estimate the probability that the expert reading panel graded the label positive for this study. The panel (two musculoskeletal radiologists, with a third settling disagreements) read the images using exactly these criteria. In each case, ambiguous or borderline findings ("on the fence") were graded as negative to favor specificity.

- acl (ACL tear): A high-grade partial or full-thickness tear of the anterior cruciate ligament, meaning complete discontinuity of the ligament, or more than 50 percent of fibers disrupted, with or without secondary signs such as characteristic pivot-shift bone contusions. Mild signal change, degeneration, or thickening without discontinuity is graded negative.
- mcl (MCL tear): A high-grade partial or complete acute tear of the medial collateral ligament, with disrupted fibers and edema within and adjacent to the ligament. Low-grade sprains and chronic or remote stress changes are graded negative.
- medial_meniscus (Medial meniscus tear): Abnormal signal that definitely contacts the meniscal surface on at least two images, or a morphologic abnormality such as a truncated, diminutive, or displaced fragment, involving the medial meniscus. Intrasubstance degeneration that does not reach the surface is negative.
- lateral_meniscus (Lateral meniscus tear): The same criteria applied to the lateral meniscus.
- medial_oa (Medial compartment osteoarthritis): A moderate or large area (roughly 1 cm or greater) of high-grade cartilage loss, defined as greater than 50 percent of cartilage thickness, in the medial compartment, with or without underlying subchondral marrow changes.
- lateral_oa (Lateral compartment osteoarthritis): The same criteria applied to the lateral compartment.
- pf_oa (Patellofemoral compartment osteoarthritis): The same criteria applied to the patellofemoral compartment.
- effusion (Joint effusion): A moderate or large amount of fluid distending the joint.
- synovitis (Synovitis): Inflammation and thickening of the synovial lining of the joint.
- bakers_cyst (Baker (popliteal) cyst): A moderate or large fluid collection in the characteristic location behind the knee.
- contusion (Contusion): A bone contusion, seen as bone marrow edema-like signal from impact, without a discrete fracture line.
- fracture (Acute fracture): An acute cortical break or fracture line.

How to answer:
- Base every estimate only on what the images show.
- Give each probability as an integer from 0 to 100. It is your best estimate and does not need to be a round number.
- Set "assessable" to false when the images do not let you judge that label (for example, the relevant anatomy is not covered or image quality is too poor). Still give your best probability.
- In "evidence", list up to three image labels (such as "S2-14") that most influenced the estimate, or an empty list.
- Keep each "note" to one short sentence.

Image conventions:
- Each image is preceded by its label: S<series number>-<slice number>.
- Slices within a series are ordered by physical position. Sagittal series run from the patient's right to left, coronal series from front to back, and axial series from foot to head.
- Unless a series says otherwise, images are shown in standard viewing orientation. Sagittal: front of the knee on the image left, head at the top. Coronal: the patient's right on the image left, head at the top. Axial: the patient's right on the image left, front at the top.
- The study header states which knee was scanned and where medial and lateral are.
